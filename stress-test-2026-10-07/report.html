The big picture

20lessons run, Year 1 to Year 6, nine subjects
19 of 20design reviews passed first time
12lessons where the worksheet was refused for not fitting
10lessons that lost at least one picture
0pages with text clipped by the page edge

The packs build. Every lesson produced slides, sheets, answers and a wall or a stated reason for none. Nothing crashed in a way a teacher would see.

The faults repeat. Most of what the checks found is the same dozen things showing up in lesson after lesson. Fix those and most of the list goes with them.

Three causes sit behind most of it.

  • The sizes the designers are told are wrong. They plan a sheet with one set of measurements and the page is built with another. That is where the refusals, the cut content and the cramped boxes come from.
  • A lost picture is not followed through. The picture goes, but the script, the card and the task that pointed at it stay.
  • Things only scale down. A figure shrinks to fit a tight page but does not grow to use an empty one.

Up to five tries on one lesson. Content was dropped to pass: a photo and a practice go (fronted adverbials), a question part (subtraction), the grouping boxes (rocks). The Year 2 clocks sheet could not be built at all until the lesson itself was changed.

One-offs on worksheets

  • Bar chart grid lines are pale grey hairlines and only run across. They may vanish on a photocopier. You asked for squared axes.
  • Two typefaces on one page wherever a tally chart sits beside a bar chart, with the two titles at different sizes and heights.
  • Fractions are stacked in sums and flat with a slash in sentences, on the same page.
  • The comma sentences children write into are the smallest text on the sheet, centred, up to 62mm from their question number.
  • The carry row under a long multiplication grid is near-white and 5.8mm tall.
  • Two shapes both labelled 12 cm drawn at 59mm and 49mm, where children compare areas.
  • Slips print two headings for one thing: "Words you can use:" then "Word bank".
  • A picture word bank was refused as "missing", so a second text word bank was added that nobody asked for.
  • A small grey note under a table at about 7pt that children need to read.
  • Several helper gaps: no bold word inside a sentence, no second line in a table heading, no line across a drawing box, no picture in a word bank chip, writing lines stop at six.

Slides

8 of 20lessons
Root causeTwo answer-slide rules contradict each other

An exact answer must use the paired reveal. The paired reveal refuses any slide where a figure changes. So an answers slide cannot show the bars shaded or the chart filled, and the deck carries a false "no reveal mark" note (on 15 slides in one lesson). In fractions the answer bars stayed blank.

6 of 20lessons
Root causeVocabulary card placement rules are too strict

A card must sit straight before the first slide showing its word, with no allowance for a starter, and a two-word card ("whole and part") can never pass. Results: a card that gives away two starter answers, a word said aloud a slide before its card, and cream word cards beside the usual green ones where designers built round it.

6 of 20lessons
Root causeA table is refused for height it was given

The layout hands away part of a table's share before drawing it. One designer spent all its tries on this and failed; the repair was a small nudge to four numbers. Elsewhere tables were turned into plain text or slides were split.

5 of 20lessons

The documented way is refused with a message that names no difference. Designers typed the numbers in by hand.

Ovals cut the first and last letters, underlines and brackets cross the tails of g and y, boxes land on the full stop, a highlight swallows the comma. Leader lines run under the next phrase so a note looks tied to the wrong words.

The designer had to mark the lesson's main body diagram "not essential" to get it on the slide at all. It printed at about 11 percent of the slide and children are asked to find a red tube on it.

Drawing faults seen on slides

  • Clock: the long hand runs out through the number it points at, so 3 and 9 are struck through and 12 reads as 1|2. On every clock, on slides, sheets and wall.
  • L-shape: the two inside side lengths are written inside the shape, where they read as the width of the leg. 16 slides, the wall and two sheets. No dashed split line, no numbered parts.
  • Part-whole model, upright: the lines run through the word "whole".
  • Coordinate grid: a point on the top or right edge has its letter cut off.
  • Labelled photo: blank write-on lines drew across the photo and off its edge.
  • Reveal mark: "A" then an answer prints as "Asource", the space is lost.
  • Option cards: the cards holding an answer printed larger than the others, so size gave the answer away.
  • Shape size: a shape changes size between a question slide and its answer slide.

Smaller slide points

  • The lightning bolt shows on one Your Turn layout and not the other. A sheet task shows the bolt, not the sheet sign.
  • Answer slides and model-answer slides have empty teacher notes because the design gives no script for them.
  • Teacher notes that say "the slide after next" are wrong once a unit is split across slides.
  • A launch's steps are meant to be a blue list and print with green number badges.
  • A portrait placed by its installed location (the known fault that breaks a deck after an upgrade).

Pictures

Read this section with care. Twenty lessons searching at once used up the hourly Unsplash allowance by 16:53, and this app has no picture generator. A single normal run would do better. The faults below are the ones that would still happen on a normal day.

HighThe script still points at a picture that never arrived

"This photograph shows a place like it, at night." beside nothing. "Look at this sponge." "These five pictures" over four pictures and a white block. The repair step is not allowed to reword the script.

Everylesson after 16:53

A sponge and a raincoat were marked final and unavailable. That triggers a lesson rework and a second review, where waiting or trying again at the end would have found them. Two picture-heavy lessons inside an hour could do this on an ordinary day.

3 of 20lessons

The easier and harder sheets would have been silently dropped on one lesson. On two others a correct sheet was refused because the record still described the old one.

The slide for "a river you have not seen" showed the estuary just taught. Nothing compares a new picture with those the lesson already has.

Both "wind" photos are the American flag. "London houses in 1666" is a modern York street with shop signs. The slate is a museum shot with a handwritten label and a scale bar. An archive stamp shows on a river photo, 50mm wide on the wall. "Abdi" is a child in a pink heart T-shirt with long hair. A set of five rocks clashes in style and size.

Genuine misses (every source working)

  • Very specific requests do not exist as stock photos: a marigold lifted out with its roots showing, a tulip growing in soil, Rama and Sita welcomed home with lamps, a firefighter spraying a hose beside a modern engine.
  • The finder was right to refuse near misses, including a painting with a bare-breasted figure and one where the wrong character held the bow.
  • Best result with the allowance working: 7 of 8 (Benin lesson). Worst: 5 of 13 (plants, Year 1), 10 of 12 after the lesson was reworked round what arrived.
  • The Wikimedia search only fetches the top three results, so a better lower-ranked picture is never seen (2 lessons).

Drawings the plugin does not have

Ten cases. Each one cost the lesson on slides, sheets and wall together.

  • Four-quadrant coordinate grid. The biggest loss in the test: five slides ask about points and shapes that are not there. A new drawing is built and waiting for your say-so to install.
  • Long multiplication on the board and wall. Only worksheets have the grid, so the teacher writes both models by hand in an empty box.
  • Exchange marks on column subtraction. No crossed-out digit or small 1, on board or wall.
  • L-shape with a split line and numbered parts.
  • Fraction wall with a ruled line down it.
  • Squared bar chart axes.
  • Ten frame (exists under another name, not listed where designers look).
  • A loop or cycle diagram.
  • A clock with only the long hand, and coloured hands without the digital time printed under them.
  • A whole river from source to mouth (needs a generator, which this app lacks).

What kept being redone

12 of 20
Worksheet designer

Refused for fit on 12 lessons, 1 to 5 tries each. Six lessons passed first time. This is the biggest time sink and it comes from the wrong measurements.

13 of 20
Slide designer

Refused at least once on 13 lessons. Most common reason: too much text for the space (up to 23 refusals on one deck). Then the table height fault, the reveal pair fault, and the word card rules. Four designers built throwaway test decks first to find the real sizes because the written sizes are wrong (a column layout "needs 2 to 4 inches" and needs 4.26).

5 times
Adaptation designer

Guessed how to write "these side by side" or "these stacked", was refused, and read the engine's own code to find out. Five lessons.

1 of 20
Design reviewer

Sent one lesson back (it did not fit its time). About eight others were approved with one or two small corrections. This step is working.

173wordings
Voice editor

About 9 changes a lesson, from 3 to 19. Its second pass over the easier and harder sheets changed 7 of 79 and can only see the first line of each instruction. Twice its change did not reach the answer sheet, so the slide and the answers now word a point differently.

3 of 20
Lesson designer

Two of its checks misfire: one needs "two new words" without saying so, one read "on the whiteboard" in a teacher note as where children write. One lesson hit the limit of tries because of them. There is no subject guide for English.

Run plumbing

  • Designers start the moment the wording is done, but a lost picture can come back minutes later and change the lesson under them.
  • A picture search sat idle for 55 minutes and nothing noticed.
  • Four leftover background jobs ran at full power for up to three hours after their workers had finished. This is what slowed your computer.
  • Workers share one scratch folder and can overwrite each other's files. A second picture top-up reuses the first one's folder names.
  • Two of the run's own instructions do not match its tools (a missing setting for the picture finder, and a check that fails unless given something its help calls optional).
  • The deck's fingerprint breaks if a file is saved a certain way (1.0 becomes 1), failing the picture check. Three lessons.

What I did differently from a normal run

  • No second review after three lost-picture reworks, to save usage after the limit was hit at 17:37.
  • No wording pass on the easier and harder sheets for the later lessons (it had changed 7 of 79).
  • The dot-placing step for labelled photos was not run. The slide designers placed dots by eye and the checks found every dot on the right feature.
  • Pictures lost only to the spent allowance did not trigger a rework. Those lessons show holes a normal run would have filled.
  • One pack that the plugin would have withheld was built anyway (Diwali printed cards) so it could be looked at.
  • L-shapes were recorded as "we can draw this" to see what the existing drawing really produces.
  • Nothing was written to your real lesson log. Everything is in the test folder's own log.
  • One look-up by a reviewer sent your email address in a request to Wikimedia. The plugin's own picture fetchers do not. Told you at the time.

What this test cannot tell you. It ran in Claude Code only, so nothing here covers Codex. Picture results are worse than a real run. Twenty runs at once is harder on the machine than you would ever be.
