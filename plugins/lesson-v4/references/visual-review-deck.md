# Deck visual review

Use this file only when `ARTEFACT` is `deck`.

Speaker notes are teacher support. Read them when they affect how the teacher
uses the visible page, but do not count text that exists only in speaker notes
as pupil-visible content.

When pupils need to see a question, instruction, option, label, source,
representation or other essential meaning in order to do the task, that meaning
must be visible on the slide. A note may tell the teacher how to use or explain
what pupils can already see; it cannot rescue essential pupil-facing content
that the rendered slide omits.

A slide that says only "Discuss this" while the actual question exists only in
speaker notes is therefore a blocking visual finding.

**What a later slide inherits from an earlier one.** A deck is one lesson, not a stack of separate slides, and later slides routinely lean on earlier ones: a recall task asks children to label lines they met three slides back, an answers slide pays off its question slide, practice questions send children to a map the teach slide introduced. This is the check that most depends on one reader walking the whole deck in order, which is why the deck is never split. Carry the audience's growing knowledge forward as you read, and when a later slide assumes something an earlier slide never actually delivered, report the pair as one fault anchored on the earlier slide - repairing what should have been shown is what makes the later task fair.

### Teacher visual-profile calibration

Read `teacher-slide-visual-profile.md` before this section.

**Card boundary.** Distinguish blank slide background from blank space trapped inside an ordinary white card. Background may be deliberate breathing room. A card that is materially taller or wider than the object it contains is a finding when the unused card area makes the object look undersized or unfinished and a tighter boundary would preserve the same content.

**Task phases.** When one visible instruction contains several consecutive actions, check whether the actions can be seen as separate phases before the whole paragraph is read. A protected source string may use line or paragraph breaks without changing its words.

**Load-bearing orientation.** When two load-bearing visuals share a row or stack, judge the smallest one. A neat row is not successful when it makes a photograph, diagram or reference materially smaller than a stack could make it in the same available area. Do not report a horizontal row merely for being horizontal; a row of two large comparable circuit diagrams is sound.

**Main task prominence.** The principal pupil task must be visible in the slide body at task-reading size. A small top-right header cue may support the body, but it must not be the only place a child can discover the main task.

**Card boundary.** Distinguish blank slide background from blank space trapped inside an ordinary white card. Background may be deliberate breathing room. A card that is materially taller or wider than the object it contains is a finding when the unused card area makes the object look undersized or unfinished and a tighter boundary would preserve the same content.

**Useful background space.** Background is not automatically good breathing room. When an important answer, reference, table or task remains materially smaller than nearby unused space and could safely become larger or better aligned without adding content, report the weak use of space.

**Task phases and attachment.** When one visible instruction contains several consecutive actions, check whether the actions can be seen as separate phases before the whole paragraph is read. When different phases govern different visible objects, the wording should be attached to the object it governs rather than collected remotely in the header.

**Whole-composition alignment.** Judge related columns and panels as one composition. A shorter group automatically pinned to the top beside a much taller peer is a finding when deliberate vertical centring or shared top/bottom alignment would make the relationship clearer without changing content.

**Load-bearing orientation.** When two load-bearing visuals share a row or stack, judge the smallest one. A neat row is not successful when it makes a photograph, diagram or reference materially smaller than a stack could make it in the same available area. Do not report a horizontal row merely for being horizontal; a row of two large comparable circuit diagrams is sound.

**Active work surface dominance.** A non-load-bearing setup picture must not receive the dominant share while the table, diagram, source or other surface pupils actively work from is left materially smaller and harder to use.

**Parallel-group distinction.** When two parallel flows or groups must remain conceptually distinct, there should be one visible group discriminator. A group accent or meaningful group heading is sufficient. Do not require multiple simultaneous discriminators.

**Success-criteria identity.** The same procedural success criteria should keep the green panel and compact white step-card identity across templates. A flat divided rendering of the same criteria beside white-card versions is a local visual inconsistency.

**Semantic colour.** The deck's grammar is asking versus telling: child-facing questions and instructions carry house blue; explanation and statements stay black; a block doing both splits at the boundary with only the asking blue. A question left black, or a whole mixed statement-plus-question block coloured blue, is a fault. Alternating blue-purple peer prompts are optional. `task-action` may expose action verbs inside a multi-phase instruction. `problem-state` red, `safety-warning` red, vocabulary green and answer green must retain their distinct meanings, and on an answer slide the answer words themselves are green, not just an outline around them.

### Photograph fit and answer attachment

Review the final rendered deck with the final photographs present. Grey placeholders can show whether the planned geometry is calm, but they cannot prove that a real photograph is readable, well cropped or visually balanced.

For `contain`, the full photograph must remain visible at its natural proportions. Blank bands are acceptable when they protect evidence or identity. For `cover`, the frame must be filled by a centred crop, not by stretching. A cover crop is BLOCKING when it removes evidence named by the photo requirement. Any photograph that looks wider, thinner, taller or shorter than the real object is BLOCKING.

On a photograph-based answer, each photograph and its own answer must read as one card. A row of photographs with one combined answer strip is BLOCKING because the child must work out which answer belongs to which photograph.

On a completed sort, check whether the item labels use the category areas confidently. Large unused category areas with small labels are MINOR when all text is readable, but become BLOCKING when the labels are not readable from the back of the room. Related item labels must use one shared size.

### Optional decoration

This layer has exactly one fault: a picture covering something a child has to
read. Text, a number, a table cell, a label, a task, an answer, a workspace or
part of a figure, obscured enough that reading it is harder than it should be.
That is BLOCKING.

Nothing else about this layer is a finding. Not whether the drawing was a good
choice, not whether a different one would have suited the lesson better, not
whether it sits slightly awkwardly, not that a slide has none and could have had
one, and not that the deck used fewer than you would have. Overlapping a card is
how this layer is built to work, so overlap by itself is never the fault: the
only question is whether something is now harder to read.

That boundary is the point of the layer. It carries no teaching and costs a child
nothing, so a reviewer's view of it is taste, and taste findings crowd out the
ones that stop a lesson working. Whether a deck uses the layer well is the
teacher's call, and the Slide Designer already reports how many pictures it
authored.

P3 has no cross-resource carry-across requirement.
