# The Test-Question Bank — real paper questions as starters

## What it is and why it matters

`[PLUGIN_ROOT]/builder/assets/test-questions/` holds real test questions cropped from past papers as individual PNG images, filed by subject and curriculum strand. Each image is the question exactly as a child will meet it in a test: the same layout, the same answer boxes, the same "1 mark" tag.

That authenticity is the whole value. A written starter question and a bank question can rehearse the same skill, but only the bank question also rehearses the *format* — reading a jug scale drawn the way the paper draws it, writing into the answer box the paper uses. When a bank question genuinely hits the starter's retrieval target, prefer it over writing an equivalent question, because the child gets the skill and the format in one rehearsal. One bank question replaces the whole question list for that starter: it is the "single rich problem" retrieval form, already a recognised starter shape.

The bank serves the starter's existing job — it does not change what a starter is for. The retrieval target still comes first (the prior lesson's skill, or the prerequisite today's LO builds on), and the bank is searched *for that target*. When nothing in the bank fits the target, or the skill being warmed needs several quick reps rather than one rich problem (number-fact fluency, quick-fire vocabulary), design the written starter as usual. A near-miss question pressed into service teaches the wrong thing; falling back is the correct call, not a failure.

## How the bank is organised

Folders narrow from subject to strand, and filenames describe the question's content:

```
test-questions/
  maths/problem-solving/year-4/measurement/capacity-jug-500ml-pour-out-150ml.png
  grammar-and-punctuation/ks2/functions-of-sentences/rewrite-statement-question-always-cheerful.png
```

The middle levels vary by subject — maths files by year group, grammar by key stage — and more subjects, types, and year groups arrive over time. Treat the folder names as the map: list the directories under `test-questions/` to see what exists today rather than assuming this snapshot.

**Year labels are a guide to difficulty, not a gate.** The bank grows unevenly, so the year group you teach may not have its own folder yet. A question filed under a neighbouring year earns its place when its content is exactly the skill your starter retrieves and its difficulty suits your class — judge by what the question asks, not by where it is filed.

## Choosing a question

1. **Name the retrieval target first** — the prior lesson's skill or today's prerequisite, as the Starter section always requires. The target decides where to look, not the other way round.
2. **List the matching strand folder(s)** and shortlist by filename — the names are written to be read as one-line summaries.
3. **Look at the actual images** for the best two or three candidates (the Read tool renders PNGs). The filename gets you close; only the image confirms the skill, the difficulty, and that the question stands alone without the rest of its paper.
4. **Work out the answer** while the image is in front of you. The answer slide needs it, and working it through is also the check that the question is sound for your class.
5. **Pick one, or none.** One question is the starter. If no candidate survives the look, design the written starter as usual.

## Recording the choice in the Lesson Design

The starter block carries two extra lines when a bank question is used:

```
Test question: [full path to the PNG]
Test question answer: [the worked answer, e.g. "350 millilitres"]
```

Keep the `Activity:` line describing what children do ("One test question on the board — read the jug scale, subtract, answer in books"), keep `Answer slide: Yes` — a test question always has a definite answer, so the answer slide follows as usual. The slide-designer reads the path, places the image readably, and builds the answer reveal in green from the answer you record.
