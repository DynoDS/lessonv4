# Modelling resource states

The lesson-designer stores `modellingState` on each source unit that contains a genuinely different modelled move. This file defines what later resource designers must provide. It describes the teaching experience and required resource state, not where the teacher must write.

## Available states

### Prepared example

Use a complete worked or finished example when inspecting the completed form is genuinely the useful teaching experience. The example must be accurate, readable and chosen for the exact learning. Do not substitute a finished example when children need to see how the process is produced.

### Live-complete helper

Provide the exact question or starting example and a large blank or partly blank representation when completing that representation is part of the model. Leave the part being learned blank or constructable; pre-fill supporting parts that reduce irrelevant load. By the end of the model, children must be able to see the completed result on the board, so the completed state always follows on the next slide as the unit's answer, delivered `answer-slide` and drawn into the same representation, never only in the notes. The teacher who completed it live skips that slide; a teacher who did not, or could not write on the board, teaches from it. The blank alone used to count as enough wherever the teacher could draw live, and a cover teacher given a Year 4 rounding deck of blank number lines had nothing finished to show the class (17 September 2026). What to write while completing it goes in the unit's `speakerNotes.onTheBoard`.

A frame may have prepared headings or headings developed with the class. Choose clear questions that guide this task, and model how its entries follow from the evidence. Live completion should reveal the thinking; an empty generic grid with unreadable evidence does not do that. A prepared frame remains valid support when children understand its purpose and still make the intended decisions.

**A live helper shows its unknowns; an empty one shows nothing.** Every drawn helper has its own way of saying "this is the part being filled": a `?` label at a mark on a number line, an empty circle in a part-whole model, a blank row in a place-value chart, a `___` in a method frame, a region whose label ends in `?`. Use it, so the board poses the question and any teacher can see what the next two minutes are for. Left completely bare, the helper is a surface waiting for someone who already knows the lesson: a Year 4 nearest-1,000 model put `Find the two multiples of 1,000 either side of 3,462` above a line with nothing on it, and a teacher meeting it cold has to invent the demonstration (19 September 2026). The limit: this is for the parts the model will fill in. Where the teacher builds the whole figure from nothing, there is no slot to mark, and a part children are meant to work out is still not pre-answered.

**Where the move is a change to something children can see, mark it on the thing itself, not only in the criteria.** The taught move often acts on a visible object: digits in a number, the ending of a word, the opening of a sentence, a label on a diagram. Marking the part it acts on inside the case (`Round 34<<6>> to the nearest 100.`) turns a described method into a demonstrated one, and the same orange mark the criteria use (`preferences.md` → Success Criteria, `<<the part to look at or decide>>`) carries it, so the criterion and the case point at the same thing. Two limits. Where the move is a judgement rather than a change to a visible part, there is nothing to mark and nothing is marked. And the mark is on the part the move acts on, never on the answer: marking what changes shows the child where to work, while marking what it becomes does the work for them.

The teacher decides how to annotate or complete the helper. Do not instruct them to use a flipchart, whiteboard, book, visualiser or another named surface.

### Question and reference

Use this state when children need a question and references visible while the teacher produces separate working. Show the exact example and useful support, including applicable success criteria. For a live written model, normally reserve a clear, usable area beside or below the reference on the displayed slide; name the intended working in the hand-off so the renderer can allow enough space. The area need not have a box or heading. Do not fill it with explanatory panels, and do not prescribe a separate classroom surface. Omit separate space when all annotation happens inside the helper, or the selected demonstration clearly uses a different medium. Do not add empty space to a read-off or prepared example by reflex.

### Physical-demonstration support

Use this state when children need to watch real equipment, hands or movement. Decide what the screen genuinely needs to support the action: nothing extra, equipment identification, setup, safety information, a diagram, steps children will later follow or a reference they will use afterwards. The screen must support rather than compete with the real action. This file does not prescribe layout, sizing, styling or title treatment.

## Choosing and handing off the state

Choose from the learning:

- If studying a completed form is the useful experience, use **Prepared example**.
- If a representation or helper is completed as part of the model, use **Live-complete helper**.
- If children need the question and references visible while separate written working is modelled, use **Question and reference**.
- If the teaching move is performed with real equipment or movement, use **Physical-demonstration support**.

Judge each genuinely different modelled move separately. Different moves in one lesson may need different states. Do not split one simple model into artificial mini-moves merely to create separate treatments, and use one shared instruction when several moves genuinely need the same state.

Generic working boxes are not a default; purposeful room for the specified live working is legitimate. Any prepared model or helper must be large, clear, readable and suitable to annotate when annotation could be useful. When the needed helper does not exist, check existing helper configurations, faithful compositions, required diagrams and required photographs. If no route preserves the state, follow the affected resource designer's named gap contract. Do not substitute an unsuitable representation and do not publish a resource that hides a load-bearing missing helper.

A separate optional draw-live pencil cue remains governed by `preferences.md`: when the lesson designer judges that a reusable reference would be useful to copy verbatim onto a working wall, the cue may suggest that possibility. It is never an instruction to the teacher and adds no child-facing or speaker-note direction.

The lesson designer normally supplies the exact modelled starting example so the lesson is ready to teach; the live teacher may substitute another suitable example.
