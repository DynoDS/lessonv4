# Adaptive Adaptation

The pedagogy behind Below and Greater Depth resource provision for a normal private-practice worksheet. Below, Expected and Greater Depth describe resource variants, not permanent types of child. Read in full whenever a lesson has a private worksheet that requires an adaptation decision.

The adaptation-designer makes separate Below and Greater Depth decisions. For Tier 1 Below and for Greater Depth, `Use Expected unchanged` is a valid deliberate outcome when the Expected resource already provides appropriate access, support and demand. Do not manufacture differences merely to create three physically different sheets. A shared frame remains one inclusive sheet and skips this process.

---

## What the lesson protects

Before any tier is chosen, name the one idea this lesson is about: the thing that, if it were taken out, would mean a child had done a different lesson. Everything else on the page is a way in, and a way in can be changed freely.

`Find 10 more than a four-digit number` protects what adding ten does to a number; the four digits are only the scale it is met at. `Represent 4-digit numbers` protects the four digits themselves, because the size is what is being taught. Two lessons a fortnight apart, opposite answers, and the whole Below decision turns on which kind this is.

Write it down before the tier decision, because every later choice is checked against it. A Below resource that no longer carries the protected idea is a different lesson whatever its objective line says. A real science Below sheet moved `To explore the importance of having a balanced diet` to `To choose different types of food and say how they help the body`: the food knowledge survived, and `balanced`, which was the lesson, did not.

---

## The Three Tiers

A Below adaptation falls into one of three tiers. The adaptation-designer selects the tier from the lesson in front of it.

**Tier 1: same objective, the way in changed.** The protected idea is within reach once reading, writing, recall load or the layout of the recording surface is lifted. Keep the class objective and change only the route to it. The Expected sheet may already do this unchanged.

**Tier 2: the same idea, entered lower and climbing.** The protected idea still holds at a smaller scale, so meet it there and walk back up inside the one resource. The sheet starts where the child can begin, and its last piece of work is a class-sized case of the same idea.

A sheet that stops at the smaller scale is the fault this tier exists to prevent. `Find 10 and 100 more or less` came back as five two-digit questions and ended there: the child practised Year 2 number for the whole lesson and never met what the class met. Built as a climb it runs `34 → 44`, then `67 + 10`, then `167 + 10`, then `1,167 + 10`, and the same child finishes on the class's own number. Record where the climb starts and the class-sized case it ends on.

Whether to climb is a teaching decision about the sheet's room, not a test of whether a route up exists. A route usually exists. It is worth walking when the class idea holds at the smaller scale, the walk is short, and no second new idea arrives with the size - then the child ends the lesson having done what the class did, which no amount of small-scale practice buys. It is not worth walking when the rungs crowd the accessible practice down to two or three questions, when the class case brings a second new idea, or when the last item is one the child could not attempt even with help. An unreachable last question teaches a child that the last question is not for them, which is the same message the parallel sheet sends.

Where the honest answer is not to climb, record `Not selected` with the reason and name what carries the connection instead: the same method, the same representation, the same vocabulary, the same context. A Below sheet working two-digit exchange on the class's own chart in the class's own words is connected whether or not a four-digit number appears on it. The relationship to the class learning is always required; the ladder is one way of carrying it, not the only one.

Keep a strong preference for the same topic or strand. Preserve National Curriculum meaning without copying statutory wording mechanically.

**Tier 3: the prerequisite, named honestly.** The protected idea does not survive being met lower down, because the scale or the concept IS the thing being taught, and the child does not yet have what it rests on. Name the prerequisite and why it is needed. The teacher or SENCO decides when and how it is used. Tier 3 still ends with one item reaching towards the class lesson, even one the child needs help with, so the resource points somewhere instead of running parallel. Where the prerequisite sits so far below that any such item would be a token nobody can attempt, say instead how the prerequisite connects to what the class did: the connection is required, the printed item is not.

**The decision test.** Ask what makes this lesson hard for a child who is behind. There are two answers and they lead to different tiers.

1. Name the protected idea.
2. **Is the way in hard, or is the idea hard?** Reading the questions, writing the answers, holding the steps, reading the layout: that is the way in, and it is Tier 1.
3. When the idea itself is hard, ask whether the protected idea still holds at a smaller scale. Yes gives Tier 2, entered small and climbing. No, because the scale is the protected idea, gives Tier 3.
4. Use a teacher-supplied working level when one exists; otherwise apply the default gap to size a Tier 2 or Tier 3 entry point.

**Start at Tier 1 and move only for a reason you can name from the lesson.** Reading and writing are the commonest barrier in a primary classroom and the only one that can be lifted without knowing anything about a particular child.

**Absence of pupil information is not a reason to lower the objective.** It was read as one, and every maths lesson in a week came back at Tier 2 carrying the same sentence: no assessment evidence was supplied, therefore the class objective could not be retained. Nothing was known about those children, so nothing about them justified a two-year drop either. A run that arrives with a year group and a topic and nothing else is the normal case, not a case with something missing. What justifies a tier is the lesson: what it protects, and whether that survives being met lower down.

Do not select Tier 1 because retaining the class objective sounds more inclusive, and do not select Tier 2 or Tier 3 because an easier version is easy to reach for.

## Scale is a maths ladder, and most subjects have no ladder

Maths has a scale to walk down: two-digit, three-digit, four-digit. That is what makes a Tier 2 climb possible, and it is also why Tier 2 gets over-chosen in maths, because an easier version is always within reach.

Most other subjects have no such ladder. There is no Year 2 version of the Romans, and a child who cannot read a page about the Nile can still understand why it mattered when someone says it aloud. So in history, geography, science, PSHE and RE the barrier is nearly always the reading and the writing, which is Tier 1: keep the objective, change the way in.

The failure to watch for in those subjects is not the wrong year group. It is the subject quietly draining out of the task until naming, matching and colouring are what is left: the history sheet that becomes a literacy worksheet, the circuits lesson that becomes colouring a bulb. The protected idea is the guard. If the Below task no longer carries it, the task is wrong however accessible it has become.

## Default Working-Level Gap

Use these defaults unless the teacher's brief names a specific working level.

| Class year group | Default Below working level |
|---|---|
| Y2, Y3, Y4 | Two years below |
| Y5, Y6 | Three years below |

Year group 1 and EYFS use the same two-year rule (so Y1 → EYFS / pre-school equivalents).

If the brief supplies a specific working level, use it exactly and note the override at the top of the output. Teachers know their pupils better than a default rule does.

## Why This Approach Is Defensible

The SEND Code of Practice (2015) describes SEN Support as "education which is **different from or additional to** that normally available to pupils of the same age." The Code, the pre-key-stage standards, the Rochford Review and the Engagement Model all exist because the DfE recognises some pupils cannot be served by age-related objectives alone. Tier 2 and Tier 3 adaptations are not lowered expectations — they are the graduated response working as designed.

The ECF's rhetoric against "differentiation by task" was written to push back against lazy three-way worksheets with drifted-down expectations and capped ceilings. It was not written to deny that some pupils need different LOs. The adaptation-designer's outputs therefore always name:

- The class LO
- The selected Below objective or prerequisite focus, according to the tier decision
- The next-step LO only when supplied curriculum, assessment or sequence information makes it real; otherwise `Not supplied`
- The connection to class learning, which may be `None`; do not prescribe live participation

Together these answer the three questions SLT, SENCos or book-looks ask: *Is the LO pitched correctly today? Is the child still experiencing the class curriculum? Is the gap closing, or just paralleling?*

## How to Select a Below Objective

Use the relevant National Curriculum programme of study to check statutory content and progression. Preserve its meaning, but do not assume that statutory wording is automatically a precise lesson objective or suitable pupil-facing wording. Record the accurate internal objective and use the established short objective form where pupils see it.

**Keep a strong preference for the same topic or strand.** When an honest prerequisite route exists within the class topic or strand, prefer it because it can preserve useful vocabulary, representations and experience. Do not force that match when another prerequisite genuinely prepares the learner better.

**Use general adaptation and subject guidance together.** This reference owns the tier, access, dignity and workload decisions. The matching subject file owns genuine disciplinary progression and depth where it exists. Neither source has blanket authority to override the other. Apply both to the actual objective and supplied evidence; when they genuinely pull in different directions, resolve the reasoned choice in the adaptation rather than casually discarding one.

### Maths

Use `subject-maths.md` alongside this reference for mathematical progression, representations, reasoning, Problem Solving and workload judgement. Preserve the class concept through Tier 1 when the evidence supports that route. Otherwise locate the nearest honest mathematical prerequisite at the supplied or default working level, keeping a strong same-strand preference while allowing a justified departure.

Do not maintain a second compressed year-by-year mathematics curriculum in this general reference.

### English

Do not use fixed generic ladders such as evaluation → inference → retrieval → fluency or composition → paragraph → sentence → word. Preserve the actual reading, writing or grammar thinking through a lighter access route when the Tier 1 evidence supports it. Otherwise choose an honest related working-level objective from the supplied text, curriculum, assessment or sequence context. Detailed English progression belongs in a future subject-English file.

### Science

Choose among:
- the same scientific concept made accessible where the Tier 1 evidence supports it;
- a genuine prerequisite concept;
- a connected working-scientifically objective where that is the honest learning need;
- Tier 3 when none of those routes is coherent.

Do not replace the intended conceptual teaching with a generic "practical version" merely because a learner is struggling. Practical equipment or activity may still form part of an adapted route when it genuinely supports the concept or enquiry skill rather than substituting for it.

### Foundation subjects

History, Geography, RE, Computing, Art, DT, Music, PSHE and MFL often do not have a simple year-by-year ladder. Use the matching subject file where one exists. Both of these routes are legitimate:

- preserve important class thinking through reduced reading, clearer chunking, visual support, a word bank, sentence stems, fewer sources or another more accessible route;
- select a genuinely lower or prerequisite cognitive demand when that is what the learner needs.

Neither route is automatic merely because the resource is Below. Keep a useful connection to class vocabulary, representations, experiences or themes where it remains honest and accessible, without forcing an identical surface experience.

## Greater Depth — same objective

Greater Depth stays on the class objective and year-group content. It deepens through authentic subject demand such as stronger relational thinking, representation choice, comparison, generalisation, justification, precision, evaluation or a more demanding decision. Do not accelerate into later curriculum content.

Fresh same-objective practice may be included when it improves case coverage, but harder numbers, extra quantity or a changed surface representation do not by themselves create Greater Depth.

First decide whether a distinct Greater Depth resource is useful. When the Expected task already provides appropriate open depth and no different support, input, criterion or demand is needed, `Use Expected unchanged` is a valid deliberate decision.

When a distinct resource is needed:

- retain or add purposeful support when it enables the deeper thinking without supplying the answer;
- remove support only when consulting it would perform the thinking being assessed;
- allow a different surface representation when choosing, translating or reasoning with that representation genuinely serves the subject learning;
- treat a new context or representation as one available depth move, not automatic evidence of depth;
- do not create challenge through extra quantity, unnecessary unfamiliarity, inaccessible reading or automatic scaffold withdrawal;
- use the amount of work the task genuinely needs. One rich task may be enough; several concise prompts may suit another lesson.

Use `reasoning-prompts.md` for reusable cognitive shapes and the matching subject file for authentic disciplinary depth. The two sources work together: the catalogue supplies a possible thinking structure, while the subject and objective determine whether that structure is genuinely deep here.

When the common task is open, Greater Depth may remain the same central task with richer input, sharper success criteria, a more demanding reference or a higher standard for the outcome. Do not bolt on extra work merely because a capable pupil may finish.

## Below — Useful connection without prescribed participation

Below has its own resource decision. It may use Expected unchanged, receive a supported version of the class objective, or receive related or prerequisite work selected through the tier decision.

The teacher decides grouping, timing, recording surface, staffing and which parts of the live lesson a pupil joins. The adaptation may identify class vocabulary, representations, experiences, themes or choices that remain useful, but it must not prescribe an attendance pattern or adult role.

Keep a connection to the class lesson where that connection is useful and genuinely accessible. Reuse meaningful vocabulary, visual language, experience or context when it helps. Do not force an identical surface experience when it creates a reading, conceptual or access barrier.

### Support and representation

Support remains when it enables the intended thinking and leaves when it supplies that thinking or answer. Fading is an important available pattern, not a compulsory template.

There is a light preference towards retaining useful visual or structural support on Below work so the sheet does not drift into a long run of plain-text questions. The support does not have to be repeated mechanically on every item. Decide from the actual task.

Provide a pre-drawn representation when:
- reading or interpreting that representation is the target; or
- the adaptation specifically decides that the structure is needed for access.

Using the Below resource does not by itself require a pre-drawn representation. When constructing the whole representation is the learning, leave that construction to the pupil unless an access decision genuinely requires otherwise.

### Low-reading-load behaviour

Treat a cautious low independent reading load as the normal design stance for Below resources unless the brief gives a clear reason to depart from it. This is a resource-design safeguard, not a diagnosis that every pupil using Below has a particular reading age.

Pictures, matching, sorting, symbols, short supported prompts, word banks, sentence stems, practical records and similar formats are useful options when they remove irrelevant decoding and preserve the intended subject thinking. They are not compulsory merely because the resource is Below.

Reduce unnecessary reading load, descriptive padding and avoidable vocabulary. Shorter wording is useful when it genuinely makes the task easier to enter; it is not the target in itself. One short sentence may be right, while two or more connected sentences may be clearer when they carry one manageable idea naturally. Keep necessary subject vocabulary and use accessible support around it. Do not turn Below wording into fragments, telegraphic prose or a mechanical short-sentence pattern.

Use phonic accessibility as a practical guide for likely weak readers, not as a rigid formal compliance test. Simplify ordinary directions where helpful. Keep essential subject vocabulary and proper nouns when the learning requires them, supporting difficult necessary words through a clear picture, labelled word bank, pronunciation cue or previously taught meaning rather than replacing them with vague alternatives.

A word bank is a visibly separate labelled support. Put `Word bank` on its own line and display each option distinctly; do not hide the choices inside the question wording.

### Pictures and visual load

A Below adaptation may request its own real photographs or other required visuals even when the Expected resource does not use them. Request them when they serve a genuine access or learning purpose, including recognition of real objects without decoding the label.

Use the smallest coherent visual set needed for the task. There is no fixed universal picture ceiling. A large or visually dense set creates real fitting risk, so name each visual's job, required size and relationship to the task. Do not ignore that risk, silently remove a required picture or replace a picture-led task with plain text.

### Amount and thinking

Below does not automatically contain more items than Expected. Choose enough rehearsal and evidence for the actual learning, likely working pace, task weight, visual load, page capacity and lesson time.

Do not impose fixed counts of fluency, reasoning or Problem Solving. Problem Solving and reasoning are available to Below when they are pedagogically sound and pitched accessibly. This resource-level choice does not weaken the separate whole-class expectation that every child should encounter worthwhile reasoning when the objective supports it. One rich task may replace separate practice and reasoning sections when it genuinely provides sufficient rehearsal, worthwhile thinking and usable evidence.

Use reasoning structures from `reasoning-prompts.md` when they fit, but rewrite the pupil-facing access route for the selected working level. Availability of a reasoning shape does not justify inaccessible wording.

### Dignity and ownership

Keep the same respectful tone and visual quality as the rest of the lesson. Do not print ability labels or use patronising framing.

Remove the access barrier while preserving meaningful pupil choice and ownership. Adapt how pupils access, gather or record information rather than unnecessarily deciding the content for them. Where completely open choice would defeat the available support, offer a short meaningful menu rather than one teacher-selected answer.

## Tier 3 — When Backward-Mapping Doesn't Fit

Raise Tier 3 when:

- the class topic has no coherent related objective at the supplied or default working level;
- no honest prerequisite can be made meaningful through the available information and task;
- the available evidence supports a prerequisite focus rather than Tier 1 or Tier 2.

A Tier 3 output names the prerequisite and explains why it is the honest focus. It defines the usable, distinct prerequisite-focused Below resource required by the Tier 3 decision. The teacher or SENCO decides when, how, with whom and with what support it is used. Do not prescribe pre-teaching, TA-led work, one-to-one delivery, intervention or a particular point in the lesson.

Tier 3 must always produce that usable, distinct prerequisite-focused Below resource.

Do not over-use Tier 3 merely because a Tier 2 design is difficult. Also do not force a weak connection to the class topic when the honest prerequisite lies elsewhere.

## Pitfalls to Avoid

**A working-level description becomes a permanent label.** Revisit the tier and objective when real assessment, curriculum or teacher-supplied review information becomes available. Do not invent a formal next-step objective or review schedule.

**The adapted resource becomes the pupil's whole curriculum.** Retain useful class language, representations, experiences or choices where they remain accessible, while leaving live participation to the teacher.

**Support becomes proxy completion.** Design work the pupil can own and complete through appropriate access support rather than having an adult perform or scribe the task.

**Accelerating Greater Depth.** Every Greater Depth task remains answerable through the class objective and year-group content.

**Stigmatising presentation.** Keep Below content respectful, coherent and visually consistent without ability labels or childish framing.

**Drift.** State a formal next-step objective only when supplied curriculum, assessment or sequence information makes it real. Otherwise record that it was not supplied rather than inventing a trajectory.

## Underlying Commitments

The whole approach rests on a small number of positions that are worth holding in mind:

- **High challenge, low threat** (Myatt). Every pupil is asked genuinely demanding questions in an environment that permits struggle and error. Greater Depth raises the demand through authentic depth. Below keeps the demand meaningful and correctly pitched rather than making the work easy.
- **Depth, not acceleration** (NCETM, Ofsted maths review). Mastery lets every child reach greater depth on today's content; acceleration produces shallow coverage that collapses.
- **Graduated response** (SEND Code of Practice). Different-from-or-additional-to provision is not lowered expectations — it's the framework working correctly. Grounded trajectory information, when available, and review make it defensible without inventing a formal next-step objective.
- **Shared experience with adapted access** (ECF, EEF). Class input, vocabulary and visual language may be shared where useful and accessible; live participation remains teacher-owned, and the adapted task need not reproduce the same surface experience.
- **Non-stigmatising scaffolds** (EEF). Any support is available to any pupil who wants it; nothing is labelled by group or ability.
