# Sixteen tasks to judge (corrected pack)

Each case gives the year group and objective, what was taught before the beat, the stimulus, the task, what the design claims for it where it says so, and one response to judge. For each case, give a verdict (PASS, REVISE, or BOUNDED CORRECTION where one small change to the key or wording would make the task sound) and a short reason.


## K01. Year 4 geography

Year 4, `To explain how water flows through a river system`. Taught: on a labelled schematic map, arrows show which way water flows, and water always flows downhill from source to mouth; the map is marked as a simple model, not a real river. The map's connections, as drawn: Stream 1 flows past village A and joins the main river at junction J. Stream 2 flows past village B and also joins at J. From J the main river flows past point X, then past village C, to junction K. Stream 3 flows past village D and joins the main river at K. From K the river flows past village E to the sea. Beat 1: children trace with a pencil where water from Streams 1, 2 and 3 goes; the teacher confirms the traced routes. Beat 2: `Oil is spilled into the river at point X. Which of the villages A to E could the oil reach? Use your traced routes.` Response to judge: "C and E, because they're downstream of X on my arrows. A and B are upstream, and D is on a stream that joins after X, so the oil can't get to them."

## K02. Year 4 history

Year 4, `To explain why Tudor children worked`. Taught before this beat: an apprentice worked for a master; the master gave food, a bed and training in a trade; being trained meant the apprentice could earn a living as a grown-up. Stimulus: a slide showing Will's case (`Will's master gave him meals and a bed. Will only ever swept the floor.`) beside a table headed `The deal` with three rows: `food ✓`, `a bed ✓`, `training ✗`. Task: `What was missing from Will's apprenticeship?` The design records this beat as the evidence that children understand why training mattered to a Tudor family, and nothing later asks about the consequence. Response to judge: "training".

## K03. Year 4 maths

Year 4, `To round three-digit numbers to the nearest hundred`. Taught and modelled: find the two hundreds the number sits between, find the halfway mark, and decide which side of it the number is on; a number exactly on the halfway mark rounds up. Task: eight numbers in no pattern (for example 462, 718, 350 and 283), each with its own number line running from one hundred to the next. On every number line, the hundred the number rounds to is already circled in red. The design records this as the independent check that each child can decide which hundred a number rounds to. Response to judge: all eight correct.

## K04. Year 4 PSHE

Year 4, `To practise a calm way to sort out a disagreement`. Taught and modelled: stay calm, say what you would like, suggest a fair way, and fetch an adult if it keeps happening or someone is hurt. Stimulus: a fictional playtime disagreement over whose turn it is with a ball. Task: `With your partner, try one line Mia could say. You can say it, write it on your whiteboard, or watch and tell your partner which part of the plan they used. You can pass.` Nobody is asked about their own experience. Response to judge: "Can we take turns? You have it till the whistle and then me."

## K05. Year 4 history

Same lesson and teaching as K02. Task: `Under the guild rules, could Will's master be punished for not training him? Use the Statute of Artificers in your answer.` Neither guilds nor any statute was mentioned in the lesson. The answer key reads: "Yes. The Statute of Artificers of 1563 required every master to train his apprentice fully, and the guild would fine a master who did not." The design records this as the lesson's most demanding historical reasoning. Response to judge: "I don't know what that is."

## K06. Year 4 science

Year 4, `To predict how the length of wire in a circuit affects a bulb`. Taught: a longer wire makes it harder for electricity to flow, so with everything else the same, the bulb in a circuit with a longer wire is dimmer. Stimulus: two circuit drawings side by side, each with one cell, one bulb and a wire. Circuit A's wire is short and circuit B's is long. The bulb symbol in circuit A is drawn larger, with yellow lines around it; the bulb symbol in circuit B is small and plain. Task: `Which bulb will be brighter? Write A or B.` The design records this as children predicting from the taught relationship. Response to judge: "A".

## K07. Year 4 history

Year 4, `To identify changes and continuities in Victorian and modern schools`. Beat A: a photograph of a Victorian classroom; children write one thing they notice about how the room is set out. Beat B, separately: an extract from a Victorian school timetable; children underline one subject that is still taught today, using their own class timetable on the wall. Beat C, separately: a photograph of a modern Year 4 classroom with tables in groups of four; children write one thing they notice about how the room is set out. No beat uses another's answer. The final task: `Using the two photographs and the timetable, write one thing that has changed and one that has stayed the same.` The design says beats A, B and C each gather evidence for the final comparison. Response to judge: "Changed: the Victorian desks were all in rows facing the front, but in the modern classroom the tables are in groups. Same: they had arithmetic like we have maths."

## K08. Year 4 English

Year 4, `To choose expanded noun phrases that create an atmosphere`. Taught: a model phrase for a spooky setting (`the crumbling house with its dark, empty windows`) beside a plain one, with the difference named as choosing details that make the reader feel something. Task: `Improve "the house". Add two adjectives.` The design records this as children revising a phrase to create a chosen atmosphere. Response to judge: "the big old house".

## K09. Year 4 science

Year 4, `To compare how well materials keep water warm`. Taught before this beat: how to read a simple results table, that warm things cool down over time, and that a higher temperature after the same time means the water stayed warmer. Children were not told which material works best. Stimulus: a supplied table for three cups of the same warm water after 20 minutes: `wrapped in wool 41 °C`, `wrapped in foil 33 °C`, `no wrapping 30 °C`. Task: `Which cup stayed warmest? What does the table tell you about wool?` Response to judge: "The wool one, it was 41. The wool kept the heat in better than the foil and nothing."

## K10. Year 4 history

Same teaching as K02. Task: sort `a hot dinner every day`, `a straw mattress by the oven`, `knowing when bread is baked just right`, `his own bakery one day` under `Helped him straight away` and `Helped him when he grew up`. Key: the baking-skill card under `Helped him when he grew up` only; acceptance condition: none. The design records it as a quick check after the deal is taught. Response to judge: a child places the baking card under `Helped him straight away` and says "because he was learning it right then, and it helped him do his job in the bakery."

## K11. Year 4 geography

Year 4, `To explain why settlements grew up beside rivers`. Taught: early settlers chose places beside rivers for drinking water, for transport and for flat land to farm. Stimulus: a map with four towns and four rivers, every town and river labelled by name. Task: `Name a town that is next to a river.` The design records this as children applying the reasons settlements grew up beside rivers. Response to judge: a child names one of the labelled towns that sits on a river.

## K12. Year 4 history

Same lesson and teaching as K02. Beat 1: children sort five cards under `What the apprentice gave` and `What the master promised`: `worked for the master`, `obeyed the master`, `food`, `a bed`, `training in a trade`. The teacher confirms the sort with the class before moving on (the expected placement and the one correction to make are in the teacher notes). Beat 2: Will's case as in K02, with no crosses or ticks on the board. Task: `Put a finger on the promise Will's master broke. Then finish the sentence: Will's family were worried, because when Will grew up...` The design records beat 1 as consolidation and beat 2 as evidence of understanding why training mattered. Response to judge: beat 1 sorted correctly; beat 2 "training in a trade... he wouldn't know how to make shoes, so he couldn't earn his living as a shoemaker."

## K13. Year 4 RE

Year 4, `To understand what light means to Hindus at Diwali`. Taught: nothing yet about Diwali beliefs; the slide is the lesson's first. Stimulus: a photograph of a family lighting diyas in a doorway, with no caption beyond `Diwali`. Task: `What does this family believe about light and darkness?` Key: "They believe light wins over darkness and good wins over evil." Response to judge: "They like pretty lights."

## K14. Year 4 maths

Year 4, `To subtract three-digit numbers using the column method with exchange`. Modelled and guided: the column method with one exchange from tens to ones. Task: eight fresh subtractions in no pattern (for example 632 − 418 and 751 − 326), recorded as independent practice of the method, steps visible beside them. Response to judge: all eight correct, exchanges shown.

## K15. Year 4 history

Same teaching as K02; the idea of a family in different circumstances has not yet been raised. Task on the board: `A Tudor family had enough food and money. They still sent their son to be an apprentice. Tell your partner: what might they have been hoping for? Then be ready to say your partner's reason.` No cards, no writing. The design records it as the beat where each child first forms a reason using the idea that training mattered for the future, on a case where hardship is not the reason, and where the teacher hears some of those reasons. Response to judge (spoken, reported by the partner): "She said they wanted him to have a proper job he could do when he's grown up, like his own shop."

## K16. Year 4 science

Year 4, `To identify conductors and insulators`. Taught: a complete circuit lights a lamp; metals tested in class (a paper clip, a key) let it light; plastic and wood did not. Task: `Will a strip of feltine let the bulb light? Explain your answer.` Feltine is an invented name that was never shown, described or tested. Key: "No. Feltine is an insulator." Teacher note: "Children who say yes still hold the misconception that all materials conduct." Response to judge: "Yes, because it might be a metal."
