## Vocabulary

disease: A disease is an illness. Some diseases spread, which means they pass from one person to another.
treatment: A treatment is what doctors do to help an ill person get better.

## Why did Victorians blame the smell?

Victorian London was crowded, dirty and very smelly.
Families lived squashed together in small houses. Toilet waste and rotting rubbish piled up in the yards, and it smelled terrible.
Nobody had a tap at home. Families got their water from a pump in the street, by pushing its handle up and down.
In 1854, a disease called cholera spread through Soho, a part of London. More than 500 people died in ten days. Doctors had no treatment that worked.
People thought the smell was making them ill, because the illness was worst where the smell was worst. So they burned tar in the streets, to make smoke they thought would clean the air.
Why do you think people blamed the smell?
Victorians thought bad air caused diseases like cholera.

## What did John Snow think?

A doctor called John Snow thought cholera came from dirty water, not bad air.
He noticed that cholera made people ill in their stomachs, not their chests. So he thought they were swallowing something, not breathing it in.
He found out where everyone who died had lived, and later marked them on this map. Each little black mark is one person.
Most of the marks are packed round one water pump, on Broad Street.
Where are most of the marks on the map?

## What stopped cholera at the pump?

John Snow got the handle taken off the Broad Street pump, so nobody could drink its water.
Soon, people stopped catching cholera from that pump.
Today, the treatment for cholera is clean water with a little salt and sugar.

## Why did the handle work when the tar didn't?

Why did taking the handle off the pump stop cholera, when burning tar didn't?
Cholera spread through dirty water from the Broad Street pump. People caught it when they drank the water.
Burning tar didn't stop it, because the smoke only changed the air, and cholera wasn't in the air.
Taking the handle off worked, because nobody could drink the dirty water any more.
The Victorians burned tar in the streets. Then they took the handle off the pump. After that, people stopped getting cholera.
