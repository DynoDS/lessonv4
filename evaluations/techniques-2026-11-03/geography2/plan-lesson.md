# Lesson 2 of the Geography long term plan

## Lesson from Kapow
2. What is the Amazon rainforest like?

## National Curriculum Statement


## Lesson Objective
LO: To describe the layers of a tropical rainforest (biomes and vegetation)

## How Kapow would teach the lesson
The lesson starts with a quiz recapping the previous lesson's knowledge about the Amazon. Then children look at photos of rainforest plants and discuss how each one has adapted to living in the rainforest, before finding out these plants make up the rainforest's vegetation, and that areas with similar vegetation are called vegetation belts, shaped by climate (the Amazon's vegetation belt is called tropical rainforest). The teacher uses a presentation to reveal the four layers of the rainforest, forest floor, understorey, canopy and emergent, one at a time, and children watch part of a 360 degree video under the rainforest canopy to spot and describe the layers for themselves. They then cut out plants and animals and stick them onto a diagram to build their own rainforest, labelled with the layer names and, if time allows, sentences using a word bank. The lesson finishes with a drag and drop activity completing a paragraph about tropical rainforests. During the week they watch another part of the Mike Leach video, this time about the dangers of the rainforest.

## Things to Look For
Children may think all rainforest layers get the same amount of light and rain, missing that each layer has quite different conditions.
Children may muddle up the order of the layers, for example thinking the canopy is the very top of the rainforest rather than the emergent layer.
Children may name a plant adaptation without saying why it helps, for example naming drip tip leaves without explaining what they're for.

## Key Questions Kapow would ask
What does vegetation mean? (All the plants that grow in a particular area.)
What is a vegetation belt? (Regions where similar plants grow, influenced by climate.)
What are the four layers of the rainforest? (Forest floor, understorey layer, canopy layer and emergent layer.)
Describe a characteristic of each layer. (Answers may include: the forest floor is wet, dark and hot, contains many minerals in the soil and has shrubs with large leaves; the understorey layer has small trees with large leaves, predators and little light; the canopy layer is cooler and lighter and creates a roof with its taller trees; the emergent layer has the tallest trees and receives lots of sunlight, rain and wind.)
Can you describe how vegetation has adapted to the rainforest? (Answers may include: drip tip leaves and smooth bark allow rainwater to easily run off; buttress roots keep tall trees stable in wet soil and strong winds.)

## Sentence Stems
The ___ layer is ___ because ___.
This plant has adapted to the rainforest by ___, which helps it ___.
A vegetation belt is ___.

## Key Vocabulary Kapow would use
buttress roots: Large, thick roots above the ground that keep tall trees stable.
canopy layer: The layer of overlapping branches and leaves below the top of the rainforest that gets sunlight, rain and wind.
emergent layer: The top layer of the rainforest with the tallest trees that gets lots of sunlight, rain and wind.
forest floor: The ground layer of the rainforest where it is dark, wet and hot.
lianas: Woody vines that wind their way up other plants to reach sunlight.
minerals: Natural substances found in the ground, not made by humans, used to make many things we use every day.
understorey layer: The layer above the forest that gets little light and is warm and damp.
vegetation: All the plants that grow in a certain area.
vegetation belts: Regions where similar plants grow, influenced by climate.

## Resources Kapow would use
It would start with a quiz to recap the Amazon rainforest, then an interactive presentation of rainforest plants for the attention grabber. The main teaching tool is a presentation revealing each layer of the rainforest one at a time, alongside a linked 360 degree video, 'Under the Canopy', that lets children look around the rainforest and spot the layers for themselves. Children would cut and stick plants and animals onto a rainforest diagram using scissors and glue, with a word bank to support labelling, and a supported version of the activity for those who need more scaffolding. A further presentation on tropical rainforests provides a drag and drop paragraph for the plenary. During the week there's a pupil video of geographer Mike Leach talking about the dangers of the rainforest.
