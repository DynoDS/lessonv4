# Fixed judgement cases: can the review tell a shallow task from a sound one?

Evaluation material, not runtime guidance. Fourteen short cases across seven subjects, each with the prerequisite teaching, the stimulus, the task and the response that has to be judged, so each is judgeable on its own. Each carries a predefined expected finding and the reason for it. The reviewer is scored against the reason, not only the PASS/REVISE label, and never sees the expected column.

**Provenance.** The cases were written by Claude (Fable 5.1) on 15 September 2026 from the matrix in the implementation plan, with the teacher's own account of the Tudor lesson as the one piece of human feedback behind them. They are not human-calibrated. The two Tudor cases (H1, H2) reuse the taught deck's content and are development material; every other case is held out from the runtime references, including `task-contrasts.md`, which uses different stimuli.

Surface forms are mixed on purpose: two sorts (one weak, one sound), two written explanations (one weak, one sound), a match, a prediction, a map task, a calculation set, a checklist-style writing task, a reflection, a scenario, a label check and an ordering. A reviewer that rejects every sort or praises every explanation fails the batch.

## The cases

### H1. History: the good/bad sort as the main evidence (expected REVISE)

Year 4, `To explain why Tudor children worked`. Taught before this beat: many Tudor children helped their households get food, fuel or money; an apprentice worked for a master who gave food, clothes and a bed but no wages; an apprenticeship could provide food and a home now and a way to earn a living later; conditions were hard and most children could not go to school.

Stimulus (board): six cards `up before sunrise`, `hot meals every day`, `only Sundays off`, `one less mouth to feed at home`, `a strict master`, `his own shop one day`; headings `Bad things about being an apprentice`, `Good things about being an apprentice`. Task: sort, then `Did the bad or the good things matter more to a Tudor family? Why?` The design records this beat as the evidence that children can explain the family's choice. Key: bad = sunrise, Sundays, strict master; good = meals, mouth, shop.

Response to judge: a child sorts all six correctly and says "the good things, because hot meals are nice".

### H2. History: the family-benefit match (expected PASS)

Same lesson, straight after the first Teach (children's work helped the household get food, fuel or money; the slide showed a girl carrying sticks and named fetching wood, carrying wheat and feeding animals). Stimulus: jobs `scaring birds off the wheat`, `selling eggs at the market`, `gathering fallen branches`; options `money for the family`, `their own pocket money`, `a fire for the family`, `food for the family`. Task: match; one card is left over; say why it does not belong. The design records this as a quick check before the apprentice chunk.

Response to judge: a child matches the three and says pocket money is left over "because the money went to the family".

### S1. Science: the prediction the drawing answers (expected REVISE)

Year 4, `To explain how the length of wire affects a bulb's brightness`. Taught: a longer wire makes it harder for electricity to flow, so the bulb is dimmer. Stimulus: two circuit drawings, one with a large bright-yellow bulb symbol and a short wire, one with a small grey bulb symbol and a long wire. Task: `Which bulb will be brighter? Why?` Key: the short-wire circuit.

Response to judge: "the left one, because it is drawn brighter".

### S2. Science: the prediction the relationship decides (expected PASS)

Same lesson. Stimulus: two identical circuit drawings, bulbs unlit, wire lengths printed as `20 cm` and `80 cm`. Task: `Which bulb will be brighter, and what in the circuit tells you?` Key: the 20 cm circuit, because the shorter wire lets electricity flow more easily.

Response to judge: "the 20 cm one, the wire is shorter so it flows easier".

### G1. Geography: reading labels as if it were the relationship (expected REVISE)

Year 4, `To explain why early settlements grew near rivers`. Taught: rivers gave water, a way to move goods, and flat land to build on. Stimulus: a map with four labelled towns and two labelled rivers. Task: `Name a town that is next to a river.` The design records this as the main practice.

Response to judge: "Ashford".

### G2. Geography: choosing a site on a new map (expected PASS)

Same lesson. Stimulus: an unlabelled map the lesson has not shown, with a river, a hilly area and three marked sites A, B, C. Task: `Which site would you have chosen to build a village? What on the map made you choose it?` Key: B (by the river on flat land); accept A with a reason about the river if flatness is argued.

Response to judge: "B, because it is next to the river so they can get water and it is not on the hills".

### M1. Maths: fresh practice with a reference (expected PASS)

Year 4, `To round three-digit numbers to the nearest 100`. Taught and modelled: find the two hundreds either side, find the halfway point, decide which side. Stimulus: eight questions with fresh numbers in no pattern (348, 762, 251, 650, 199, 505, 874, 449), each with a number line drawn from the lower to the upper hundred and no halfway mark, the three steps in a panel beside them. Task: round each.

Response to judge: all eight correct, with the halfway mark drawn in each time.

### M2. Maths: support that supplies the decision (expected REVISE)

Same lesson. Stimulus: the same eight questions, each number line drawn with the target hundred already circled in green. Task: round each.

Response to judge: all eight correct.

### E1. English: a count mistaken for the quality (expected REVISE)

Year 4, `To write a setting description that makes the reader feel the place`. Taught: a model sentence beside a flat one, the difference named (showing detail versus telling the reader the feeling). Stimulus: a picture of a dark forest. Task: `Write three sentences about the forest. Include at least two adjectives in each.` The design records this as the main practice.

Response to judge: "The big dark forest had tall green trees. The scary cold path was long and muddy. The old brown owl sat on a high branch."

### E2. English: a concise drill of the taught move (expected PASS)

Year 4, `To use fronted adverbials`. Taught and modelled: the adverbial moves to the front and takes a comma. Stimulus: four plain sentences. Task: `Add a fronted adverbial to each sentence.` Recorded as guided practice before the writing.

Response to judge: "Later that night, the fox crept into the garden."

### R1. RE: an opinion in place of understanding (expected REVISE)

Year 4, `To understand why a carol matters to a Christian`. Taught: Layla sings it because of Jesus and because her family sings it every year; Ethan sings it for the family tradition only; you cannot tell which from watching. Stimulus: a photograph of a family singing. Task: `Do you like singing carols? Why?` Recorded as the evidence for the objective.

Response to judge: "Yes, because it is Christmassy and fun."

### R2. RE: reflection that uses the taught meanings (expected PASS)

Year 4, `To explain what Christmas means to me and to a Christian`. Taught: the two meanings above. Task: `Write what Christmas means to you, then one thing that is the same or different from what it means to Layla.` Recorded as the intended personal outcome.

Response to judge: "Christmas means seeing my cousins. That is the same as Layla's family bit, but she also has the Jesus part and I don't."

### P1. PSHE: "be kind" in place of the taught decision (expected REVISE)

Year 4, `To know how to keep a boundary online`. Taught: three sentences that keep a boundary clearly and kindly (`I don't want to share that`, `I'm going to ask an adult`, `Please stop sending me those`). Stimulus: a scenario the class discussed during the Teach. Task: `What should Sam do?` Key accepts "be kind and tell someone".

Response to judge: "Sam should be kind."

### P2. PSHE: order the steps on a fresh worry (expected PASS)

Same lesson. Taught: stop, don't reply, tell a trusted adult, keep the message. Stimulus: a new scenario (a message from someone Priya does not know asking where she lives). Task: number the four step cards in the order Priya should do them. No child writes about themselves.

Response to judge: 1 stop, 2 don't reply, 3 tell an adult, 4 keep the message.
