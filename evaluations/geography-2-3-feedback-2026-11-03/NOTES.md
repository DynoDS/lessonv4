# Geography lessons 2 and 3: Daniel's feedback traced (3 November 2026)

The lessons were a mix: plugin workers for every stage, but driven by hand, first drafts written by a general helper reading the designer's instructions, a revision steered by a list of points from me, and Lesson 3's plan largely rewritten by me (scratchpad `revise_l3.py`) before the plugin's reviewer and voice editor. Each point below says where it came from.

## Patterns, most general first

1. **Several new things taught before children use any.** Lesson 2's first Teach carries the layers, the canopy and the emergent trees (seven slides before the first Do); the second carries the light, the understorey and the forest floor. Plugin's own. The rule already says to teach and use each unfamiliar member of a set before the next (`preferences.md`, the paragraph beginning "What counts as one coherent idea"). The designer judged pairs as one chunk ("two chunks of teaching (the top two layers, then the bottom two and the light)"), and the reviewer considered the split and kept it: "That would split two names that teach better together". Knock-on: word cards are attached to a Teach unit, not a slide, so vegetation, canopy and emergent arrive together before a four-slide Teach that uses one of them on its first slide.
2. **Dos that do not use what was just taught, and Dos that are light.** Lesson 3's "what would you need every day?" follows a Teach about who lives there and uses none of it. The unsteered draft had the same Do, so it is the plugin's, likely from "Where the lesson is going to hand over a set, let the class build it first". The A or B choice is marked as a task but is a discussion. Lesson 2's sort is split: the cards' facts on one slide and the layer headings on the next.
3. **Pictures that show the thing, not the learning.** Lesson 2 slides 6, 7 and 11 (a photo of the canopy where a highlighted layers drawing would show what the sentence says; the layers drawing missing where children are asked to predict what is under the canopy); slide 22 discussion with no picture while slide 23 writing has one. Lesson 3 slide 6 (one photo makes two boys "the Indigenous people"), slide 8 (the Tikuna arrive as a word on a map), slide 11 (the stilt legs are not zoomed in on), slide 4 (Yanomami label not picked out). Slide designer and designer.
4. **Question asked, then not answered next.** Lesson 2 slide 13 gives the light rule before the answer to "what is it like under the canopy?", which only comes on slide 14. Lesson 3 slide 8 asks "why build by the river?" and moves on. Daniel's order: what I want them to know, because, so, example; a cover teacher asks, then tells.
5. **Beats that do not serve the objective.** Lesson 2's fallen tree and prediction (kept partly because my revision brief said to keep it); Lesson 3's north or south question (plugin's, added in revision or review). Lesson 2's park-shade opening was also kept by my brief; Daniel asks whether it is the best way in and whether it is too simple.
6. **Lesson 3's story.** A tour of two named peoples, in the order Yanomami, Tikuna, Yanomami, so children may not link either to "Indigenous peoples" or to how people live there. Daniel's story: people live in the rainforest; compare with our lives; they need things; one way is the river, one way is the forest. Mostly mine: the unsteered draft told that general story with an unnamed village, and my brief brought the two peoples back.
7. **Small.** Lesson 2 slide 9's starred purple line ("Like the tree in the park, the canopy catches the sunlight first") is marked like sticky knowledge but is not the lesson's sticky sentence. Lesson 2 slide 13's layers drawing does not pick out the understorey.

Length: fine for both (Daniel).

## Pattern 1 investigated: why the designer grouped the layers

Daniel: this is about the plugin's decisions, not this lesson. Teach, then children use it straight away, is one of his biggest principles.

- **Not new, not caused by my steering.** Three Lesson 2 designs (the old one before 3 November, the unsteered test draft, the rebuilt one) all made the same two chunks: "the top two layers, the lower two with the light". The old one's reason: "Each layer is a name and a few lines, not a lesson of its own".
- **Two rules in the same paragraph pull opposite ways.** `preferences.md` → `Judge the workload of Dos and the final task` says a new idea is used before the next arrives, and also that a lesson usually holds "one or two pieces of teaching, each checked by a quick Do or a task" and "Three chunks of teaching with a final task on top is usually too much". `do-beats-in-a-row.py` prints the chunk count with "Usually one or two". An objective with four unfamiliar members cannot meet both, so the designer pairs them. The paragraph forbids folding a second idea into one Teach to stay inside the count, but offers only "fewer things" or two lessons, and the objective needs all four layers.
- **The escape it used drops its own condition.** "The one grouping is a set of names learned together ... each checked by a quick Do as it arrives: that stays one chunk." The designer took "one chunk" and left out "each checked as it arrives".
- **The reviewer reasoned the same way:** it weighed putting the photo check between canopy and emergent and kept them together as "two names that teach better together" and "one story".
- **Changed (uncommitted):** the amount paragraph in `preferences.md` (heavy means long telling and many tasks; several named things are each kept short and used as they arrive; a teaching point is an idea worth using, not a sentence; "they teach better together" is not a reason alone), the set-of-names exception, the quick-Do line, the designer's chunk-count read (plus: name what the children would lose wherever two new things share a Teach), and `do-beats-in-a-row.py`'s printout. 3178 tests pass.
- **Unsteered rerun** (`geography2-unsteered/`, real lesson-designer, no brief from me): each layer its own short Teach and a use before the next, one use per layer, not per fact. The opening and the emergent layer still share the first Teach (the scaffold refuses two Teach beats in a row). It drafted about 64 minutes and trimmed boards to about 54. One run only.
- **My 3 November wording widened it a little** (see below)

## Pattern 3 investigated: pictures that show the thing, not the learning

- **The opening question of the picture rules sets the floor as the target.** `preferences.md` → `Lesson Designer visual-need boundary` opens on `does this name something that exists in the world?`, says the picture "earns its place on that alone, and nothing has to be decided from it", and calls `could a child work the learning out from this picture?` the wrong test (the RE ruling: a picture need not prove anything). So the plugin asks for a photo of the canopy for a sentence about the canopy, which Daniel calls fine but not helpful: he asks whether children could look at it and see what the words say (the canopy picked out on the layers drawing beside the photo).
- **The better rules already exist and lost:** `Put on the slide what the class has to think with` (L2 slide 11's prediction without the layers so far; L2 slide 22's discussion without the drawing slide 23 has); `The picture is there whenever its thing is being talked about` and `slide-designer.md` → `Keep a unit's picture in front of the class` (L3 slide 8 introduces the Tikuna on a map only, their photo a slide later); `subject-geography.md` → `Represent a place fairly for the claim being taught`, "broader claims need broader evidence" (L3 slide 6: "hundreds of Indigenous peoples" shown by one photo of two boys); orientation, "children see what the topic is ... before anything abstract" (L2 slide 6: the layers drawing before any photo of the rainforest on a Teach board).
- **Origins:** the designer chooses which pictures each beat gets; the slide designer places them and splits beats into slides. L3's Teach wording is largely mine; its picture placement is the slide designer's. My revision briefs said "Every sentence on a Teach board that names something real should have its picture", which pushed the same floor.
- Smaller: L2 slide 13's drawing does not pick out the understorey; L3 slide 4's map does not pick out the Yanomami; L3 slide 11 reuses the whole village photo where the stilt legs want a close look.

## Pattern 4 investigated: a question asked, then not answered next

- **The rules work per slide; the fault is across slides.** `preferences.md` → `The slide carries the takeaway ... answers the question the title asked` and `teaching-sequence-content-based.md` → `Write the sentence that answers the question` both judge one slide's title against its own key sentence. Neither covers a question put to the class on one slide whose answer the next slides postpone.
- **Lesson 2 slides 11 to 14.** The design's second Teach opens on `What do you think it's like under the canopy?`, then its headline is the lesson's sticky reason (`The canopy catches nearly all the sunlight, so the lower you go, the darker it gets`), and the direct answer (the understorey, dim, huge leaves) is the explanation's next line. The word card for `understorey` was placed "straight before the next Teach", whose first slide is the question, and the slide designer put it between the question and the answer. So: question, word card, a reason, then the answer.
- **Lesson 3 slide 8.** Titled `Why build a village by the river?` but introduces the Tikuna on a map; the question is put on slide 9 and answered on slide 10, which Daniel likes. Slide 11 tells why the homes stand on legs where asking first would have let children reason it (a cover teacher would). Wording mostly mine; slide split the slide designer's.
- **Daniel's explaining order already in the plugin:** `teaching-sequence-content-based.md` line 180, takeaway, because or so, example, back to the point. The designer read "takeaway" as the lesson's general rule rather than the answer to the question just asked.

## Pattern 5 investigated: beats that do not serve the objective

- **The fallen tree (Lesson 2 slides 18 to 20).** In every Lesson 2 design: the old one (as an Apply), the test draft, the rebuilt one and the unsteered rerun. Not from the plan or any rule example. It is the designer's own use of the listed Do form "change one condition and say what follows" (`preferences.md` → `Name what the chunk needs children to do with it`), justified each time as "no new idea" and "the one unaided use of the reason". It does the work of lesson 4 in the plan ("To explain why tropical rainforests are changing"), and the wall designer even pointed at lesson 4's canopy gap. `Scope comes from the objective` already warns of "a case ... brings in an idea the objective does not name"; the designer's test was "is it a new idea?", not "does today's objective ask for it, or does a neighbouring lesson own it?". My revision brief also said to keep it.
- **North or south of the Equator (Lesson 3 slide 4).** The plugin's lesson designer, in its revision run, replaced my key question with it, with no recorded reason. The map with the Equator was in my rewrite. Likely pulled by `teaching-sequence-content-based.md` → `Write the sentence that answers the question`: "Beside it the class needs the thing it is about, a question that makes them look at it": a question chosen to make children look at the map, not about what the slide teaches. `Choose key questions for the understanding they reveal` already says otherwise.
- **The park shade opening (Lesson 2 slides 3 and 4).** Kept by my revision brief. The unsteered rerun opened instead on a puzzle from lesson 1: the Sun is strong in the Amazon all year, so why is the ground nearly dark? Daniel's question (best way in, too simple?) is a judgement on this lesson rather than a plugin fault.

## Pattern 6 investigated: Lesson 3's story

- **Three designs compared.** The old plugin design (before 3 November): the Yanomami as the one story, the Tikuna as one comparison board, plus a map question about which community lives where. The unsteered test draft: a general story with an unnamed village (imagine living here and what you need; the river gives water, food and the road; the forest gives homes, food and medicine; describe). That is the shape Daniel describes, but with no real people. The rebuild: my brief ("bring the real peoples back") and my rewrite toured two peoples, Yanomami, Tikuna, Yanomami.
- **The plugin's rule already describes his story:** `preferences.md` → `Where the lesson is going to hand over a set, let the class build it first` ("the rest of the lesson goes down their list rather than yours"). My rewrite organised the lesson by people instead of by the ways people get what they need.
- **What no rule says:** when the objective is about how a group lives or uses a place in general (Indigenous peoples), the story follows the ways the objective asks children to describe, each real example comes in where it shows one of them, and the example is joined to the objective's group by name each time it appears. The geography file has `Represent a place fairly` but nothing on examples serving a general claim.
- **Recurring plugin habit:** a map question about where a community sits (old design and rebuild), asked without a job (see pattern 5).
- **Lesson for my own process:** three of the things Daniel disliked (the park opening, the fallen tree, the tour of two peoples) came from my revision briefs. A list of points from me to the designer is steering, not testing.

## Pattern 2 investigated: Dos that do not use what was just taught, or are light

- **"What would you need every day?" (Lesson 3 slide 7).** The plugin's own (in the unsteered draft too). It is the example sentence of `preferences.md` → `Where the lesson is going to hand over a set, let the class build it first`, copied word for word. That technique asks for the set *before* teaching it, but the designer put it in the Do slot after "Who lives in the Amazon rainforest?", so the Teach before it (Indigenous peoples, schools, phones) got no use and the task felt random. The technique is sound where the scene is set; it is not that Teach's Do.
- **A or B (Lesson 3 slide 12), marked deeper (book) but a discussion.** The plugin's own (unsteered draft too). The expected answer is the river Teach said back ("water, fish and a way to travel"). `do-beats-in-a-row.py` printed "words of that answer the teaching already said: 14 of 23" and a LOOK AGAIN; the designer and reviewer read past it. `preferences.md` → `Choose Do activities for the learning` already says printing and minutes do not make a Do deeper; the choice was made on the printed sheet and six minutes.
- **The sort split over slides 16 and 17 (Lesson 2).** Slide designer. Five picture cards with a sentence each plus four headings did not fit, so it put the pictures and facts on one slide and the sort board, with names only, on the next. `slide-designer.md` → `Show a unit's stimulus once` already says a task sits on the slide that shows what children answer from, and two slides are right only when the second no longer needs it. The sort-board reference in `templates.md` says, when cards do not fit, "split by complete groups across two slides or give it a taller zone", which the designer turned into splitting the facts from the task. ("A simple idea that leads straight into the next can run on with it"), but the grouping predates it.
