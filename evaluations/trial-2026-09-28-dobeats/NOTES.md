What round 1 still showed:
- Nearly every Do beat became "decide, then tell your partner why": one habit swapped for another.
- No quick recall at all, and no Do reached back to an earlier chunk.
- Drawing or marking once in three lessons; no picture-choice beats.

Research (scratchpad dobeat-research.md, 23 formats, 16 failure modes, sourced) found the matching gaps:
guided retrieval for this age (Karpicke 2014: blank recall about 10%), drawing works with a partly drawn frame (Van Meter and Garner 2005; g = 0.69 across 53 studies), picture answer options cut language load most (Noble 2020), and an immediate check is not retrieval: reaching back after some forgetting is (Roediger and Karpicke 2006).

## 29 September: the teacher's correction and rounds 6 to 7 (trial-2026-09-29-dobeats-f and -g)

His correction: "quicker doesnt always mean effective"; the aim was removing reading and writing only where it is a barrier; finish-the-sentence and card sorts children read are still good; "A or B ... could also be easily guessed"; claims should sometimes be fully right; a generic frame `I think Jack is ___ because ___` "doesnt help them at all".

Changes:
- preferences rule renamed and rewritten: `Make every child do the thinking, and take away only the reading and writing that is not the learning` (production over recognition; a two-way choice only when the choice is the hard part; guess test; reading and writing stay when they are the thinking).
- reviewer: no check passed by a lucky guess between two options.
- lesson-designer claims: keep right, wrong and half right in the mix; when either would serve, make it right outright; use a named claim only when a voice matters, otherwise put the misunderstanding into the task (near-miss card, near-miss picture, prediction); a claim asking for a reason gets a starter on the case's own evidence or the guiding question plus two or three taught words, never a generic frame and never one that picks the verdict.
