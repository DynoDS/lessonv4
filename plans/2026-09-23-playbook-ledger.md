**Snapshot.** The plugin's working tree on 24 September 2026: lesson-v4

working tree where the quote starts. Every «quoted» passage is the file's own
words; `streamline-tools/check-ledger-quotes.py` checks each one.

**What counts as the playbook here.** Everything the run is told about running
itself: `skills/make-lesson/playbook-lite.md` whole, phase by phase, and
`skills/make-lesson/SKILL.md`; the successor lines the runtime program prints
at the end of each slice (`scripts/make-lesson-runtime.py`), because the run
reads them as instructions; the two developer commands
(`commands/edit-templates.md`, `commands/install-helper.md`); the references the
playbook sends a run to (`brief-gap-protocol.md`, `revising-in-place.md`,
`lesson-from-plan.md`, `cloud-delivery.md`, `computer-setup.md`,
`helper-route.md`); what the run programs enforce that the playbook also says
(`run-fixed-resource.py`, `validate-run-report.py`, `deliver_files.py` and the
other scripts the playbook names); and the test that caps the playbook's size.
After the check, also the lines in the designers' own files that tell the run
what to hand them or promise something about the run's report, since a fold of
the playbook has to see them.
The rows are about the run: the order of phases, what runs in parallel, what
blocks, what each agent is handed, repair rounds, what is delivered and where,
what a flagged finding does, and the report.

**What belongs to a neighbouring topic.** The review step (Phase 1.25) is listed
in full by the design reviewer list (`RV-`, group R), which gave the playbook
its wording; those rows are here marked "shared with RV" and name the RV row.
A teaching rule written into the playbook is marked shared with the topic that
owns it: worksheets (`WS-`), success criteria (`SC-`), assumed knowledge
(`AK-`), the rest of preferences (`PF-`), starters (`SA-`), subject files
(`SJ-`). The brief-gap protocol is mostly listed by the preferences, success
criteria and worksheets lists; its paragraphs are all here, the ones those lists
hold marked shared. The designers' own work (slide, worksheet, wall,
adaptation, stick-in, decorator, image scout) is topic 9; the rows added from
their files are marked shared with topic 9.

## Decisions for Daniel

Seven questions, each a real pull between two places, a rule I think is wrong,
or a fault whose fix would change how the run works. Each says what the plugin
says now, what I think and what I suggest; "yes" takes the suggestion.

1. **A repair that changes what children read** (the blocked Part D).
   - **What it says now.** When a slide or sheet needs repairing after the
     lesson was redesigned, the run always sends the quick repair version of its
     designer. That version may not write or remove anything children read, so
     it hands the job back and the round is wasted. History 5 lost two rounds
     this way, one of them 44 minutes.
   - **What I think.** The one-line fix is right: send the full designer when
     the change is to what children read. On its own it would still fail. Every
     repair has to come back with a check that no new words reached the child's
     page, only the quick versions know that check, and a repair whose whole job
     is new words for children can never pass it.
   - **What I suggest.** When the change is to what children read, the full
     designer does the repair. It edits its file in place like every repair and
     says what it changed and what it kept, but it is not asked for the "no new
     words" check: rebuilding the resource and running its own check confirm it,
     as they do for every repair. The room comes from the tidy above (settled
     items a, d, e, h and i), about 2.8 KB.
   - **Honest note.** The full designer reads its whole instructions first, so
     the repair is slower than a quick one, but it can finish.
   - **Question:** yes?

2. **The template editor pushes to GitHub on its own.**
   - **What it says now.** After you move a slide layout in PowerPoint, the
     template editor copies your changes back into the plugin, then commits and
     pushes straight away. The helper installer does the opposite: it stops and
     waits for you, because a push puts the change in front of every lesson.
     Your own notes point both ways: your note for plugin work says "After
     making changes here, commit and push to GitHub" (written for the older copy
     of the plugins), and this tidy's own rule is that nothing is pushed until
     you say.
   - **What I think.** The two jobs differ. Nobody has seen a new helper's
     drawing, so the installer is right to stop. A layout you dragged yourself is
     nearer done, but the step that copies your changes back has lost edits
     before, and you never see the rebuilt demo before it goes live.
   - **What I suggest.** The template editor shows you the rebuilt demo and
     pushes on your yes, as the installer does.
   - **Question:** should it stop and ask before pushing?

3. **Watching the agents work.**
   - **What it says now.** On Claude Code your own settings already launch the
     lesson agents where you can watch them, as panes you arrow through; that
     part is settled by your words. Your settings also say the lesson skill asks
     for this; it does not. Codex shows its agents in its own list either way.
     The run's speed rules assume each agent reports back the moment it
     finishes, so one branch moves on while another is still working.
   - **What I think.** Watched agents may make the run wait until every agent
     launched together has finished before it moves on. I have not measured it.
     Writing your wish into the skill makes that trade visible rather than
     silent, and makes the line in your settings true.
   - **What I suggest.** One line in the skill: on Claude Code, launch the
     lesson agents where the teacher can watch them, each under its role name,
     accepting that the run waits for each group launched together.
   - **Question:** yes?

4. **Two notes to the build log.**
   - **What it says now.** After the review, the run is told to add the
     review's corrections and your remaining choices to the build log. But the
     skill says the log's location is found only at the end of the run, and the
     end-of-run step says the log is for engine faults: a check that refused a
     good lesson, a picture the engine could not draw, two rules that disagreed.
   - **What I think.** They are two kinds of note. The reviewer's own small
     corrections are kept in the review file in the lesson's working folder; the
     run report carries only the findings sent back to a designer and any left
     unresolved.
   - **What I suggest.** One log step, at the end, for engine faults only.
     Honest note: the log would stop collecting the reviewer's teaching
     corrections, which would then live only in each lesson's review file; if
     you read those in the log, say no.
   - **Question:** yes?

5. **One fault that loses a whole wall or stick-in pack.**
   - **What it says now.** A deck or a worksheet pack that its one repair cannot
     fix still reaches you, with the problem flagged (your "flag the slides and
     deliver it"). Three things are still lost whole after their one repair: a
     card kit whose cards do not match the sort, a working wall that will not
     build, and an ordinary stick-in pack where one piece cannot be drawn.
   - **What I think.** The card kit is different: wrong cards on the tables
     would teach the wrong sort, so leaving it out is right. The wall and the
     stick-in pack have the deck's shape: one small fault costs the whole
     resource. The 14 September wall was lost over one worked-example step two
     characters too long (108 against 106). On 16 September you said of a
     withheld deck, wall and stick-ins: "i dont want this happening again".
   - **What I suggest.** The card kit stays as it is. The wall and the stick-in
     pack ship like the deck: the card or piece that failed is left off and
     named in your flags. Honest note: the wall and stick-in builds cannot do
     this yet; each would be built as its own change, as the sheets' was.
   - **Example.** A stick-in pack of four pieces where one number line cannot be
     drawn: today you get none of the four; with this you get three and a flag
     naming the fourth.
   - **Question:** yes?

6. **Feedback on a lesson already made.**
   - **What it says now.** The guidance says that when you react to a built
     lesson ("the answer on slide 6 is wrong", "rename the character to Maya"),
     the run edits just those parts and rebuilds, and never re-runs the
     designers, because a re-run re-chooses every example and can drop slides
     you were happy with. But nothing sends the run to that guidance when your
     feedback arrives. The lesson skill is described to the host as being for
     new lessons, so in a new conversation your feedback may not start it at
     all; and if it starts as a fresh lesson, it sets the old working folder
     aside and begins again, the very thing the guidance forbids.
   - **What I think.** It is your rule and it is never read at the moment it is
     for. One ruling of yours is also missing from it: on 14 September you
     called out a partial edit and asked for every resource to be edited, not
     only the slides being discussed (from my notes of that day; it is not in
     the plugin).
   - **What I suggest.** The skill's description names feedback on a lesson
     already made as well as new lessons, and its opening says: when your
     message is feedback on a built lesson, read the edit-in-place guidance and
     edit, do not start again. The guidance gains one line: a change you ask for
     lands on every resource that shows it, and nothing else changes.
   - **Example.** "Rename the character to Maya": the slides, the notes, the
     worksheet and the answer key all say Maya, and every other slide stays
     exactly as it was.
   - **Question:** yes?

7. **Four places where one step of the run does not meet the next** (new,
   found by the check).
   - **What it says now.**
     - A labelled photo on the stick-in pack is never corrected against the real

     - With no browser on the computer, the sheets, the wall and the stick-ins

     - The step that proves each picture's licence is skipped when the lesson

   - **What I think.** Each is a real fault a run can hit, rare but silent. None

   - **What I suggest.** Fix them as their own small release straight after this

   - **Example.** A Year 4 science lesson labelling a flower: the stick-in piece

   - **Question:** yes?
