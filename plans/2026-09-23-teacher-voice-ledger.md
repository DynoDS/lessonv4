## Decisions for Daniel

Eight questions, each a real pull: two of your rules that disagree, a ruling of
yours no instruction carries, or a gap in the guidance. Each says what the plugin
says now, what I think and what I suggest; "yes" takes the suggestion.

1. **Two parts of the voice guide nobody is sent to.**
    - **What it says now.** The voice guide sends a writer to a part for each kind of writing: questions, definitions, model answers and so on. Nothing sends anyone to the part on sentence stems and other support, or to the part on tone for each kind of lesson (a practical lesson, a sensitive one, an easy factual one). The reviewer opens them only when a line already sounds wrong. (A07, M05, G01 to G04, I11 to I14)
    - **What I think.** The questions part already says a stem is built from its question's own words, so that rule does reach writers. What never reaches them is the stems part's own examples and its limit on support: help with the structure, never the answer.
    - **What I suggest.** Writing a sentence stem or other support opens that part, and the tone part is read once, when the kind of lesson is settled.
    - **Example.** A Year 4 stem for `Why won't this circuit work?` would be written with the guide's own pair in front of the writer: `The circuit will not work because...`.
    - **Question:** yes?

2. **The chipped tooth: which sentence comes first.**

## P. The no-em-dash rule: where it is written and what enforces it

No em dashes is your rule for all your writing; your own instructions to Claude carry it for everything you read, including messages to you. Inside the plugin it is written in four places: its home in Written Voice (P01), the voice guide's avoid list (J13) and ownership line (A04, which names "the em dash rule" as Written Voice's), the guide for writing a subject file (M43), and the reviewer's note that the program has it covered (P02). Nothing else in the instructions repeats it: the agents meet it through Written Voice. It is also the one voice rule the program enforces completely: the lesson design is refused if any string carries an em or en dash, and every builder turns any dash left in a slide, sheet, wall or stick-in file into a spaced hyphen before print. What the program does not reach is the instruction files themselves, whose own examples still carry long dashes (see Out-of-date text).
