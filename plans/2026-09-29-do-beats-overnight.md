# Do beats: what happened overnight (28 to 29 September 2026)

Nothing is committed or installed in Codex. Everything ran on Claude only. The detail is in `evaluations/trial-2026-09-28-dobeats/NOTES.md`, with the research (`dobeat-research.md`) and the blind judge's verdict (`judge-verdict.md`) beside it.

## The short version

- **Do beats now test the subject, not reading and writing.** Children show the thought in the lightest way that proves it: a letter, a tick, a circle on a picture, a placed card, a drawn arrow, or saying it to a partner. Writing stays where writing is the point: the main task, an explanation lesson, recording a method.
- **Every child commits before talking.** A quick check has each child write a letter, mark or word first. Only then do they talk, so one partner can't think for two.
- **Variety is checked.** The lesson designer now reads its Do beats one after another before finishing, and changes one if they all answer the same way.
- **An independent judge preferred the new Do beats in 6 of 8 lessons**, without knowing which was new. Both of its "clear" preferences were for the new versions.

## What children now do in Do beats (real examples from tonight's lessons)

- **Geography, a river's journey:** "Which end of the Thames is its source: A or B?"; an unseen photo, "source or mouth? Find two clues"; "Where is the river carrying the most water: A, B or C?"
- **Science, food chains:** build a chain from two facts and decide which way the arrows point; "Which chain is missing its start?"; "Which animal is both predator and prey?"
- **History, Anglo-Saxons:** a real Anglo-Saxon brooch as evidence of who came; "Ava says they came because the soldiers left. Right, wrong or half right?"; the teacher reads four cards and every child writes W (wanted to come) or C (could come).
- **RE, baptism:** "What do the white clothes show?", a symbol the lesson never taught, read using what the water shows.
- **PSHE, online worries:** "Would writing back make it better or worse?"; sort who is a trusted adult; "Which half of Zoe's plan is right?"

## What changed in the plugin (in plain words)

1. **The lightest way that proves it** (preferences): the main rule above, with its limits. Reading a history source or an RE extract stays, because that reading is the subject.
2. **Quick reasons are said, not written**, and only when the reason separates understanding from a guess.
3. **Commit before talk** (preferences, and the reviewer's checks).
4. **A later Do reaches back** to an earlier idea from the same lesson. That's where real recall comes from.
5. **Three new activity types in the catalogue:**
   - **Finish the picture:** a partly drawn frame, never a blank page.
   - **Circle the clues** on a new photo.
   - **Which picture shows it?** Picture options, which cut reading the most.
6. **Subject examples** now say "tell a partner the reason" instead of "write the reason". History gains "circle it in the picture", science gains "draw the arrow" and "which picture shows it?", and geography gains "circle the clues".
7. **"Is Jack right?"** A character on their own is no longer wrong by default. Tonight's lessons had Grace, Jack, Amira, Ava and Leo "partly right" or "half right".
8. **The designer's final check:** read the Do beats in a row, and if they all answer the same way, change one.
9. **The reviewer's extra question:** could a child who reads or writes slowly still show they understood?

## How it was tested

- **15 lessons across six kinds:** history, geography, science, RE, PSHE, and a map skills lesson. Each went through the lesson designer and then the reviewer.
- **Five rounds.** Each round's problems led to the next change:
  - **Round 1:** writing in quick Do beats fell from 4 of 7 to 1 of 8. But "tell your partner" became the new habit.
  - **Rounds 2 and 3:** the research changes went in, and new topics produced the best lessons (river, food chains). Two reviewers disagreed about talk-only checks, so the fix moved to the designer.
  - **Round 4:** baptism fixed. The Anglo-Saxons lesson was still "write, then tell your partner why" four times, and the reviewer caught it.
  - **Round 5:** the Anglo-Saxons lesson fixed, and a control lesson (online worries) was rebuilt to make sure nothing broke. Both approved.
- **All tests pass**, apart from the same 8 old failures as before.

## Honest notes

- **Two lessons lost to their old versions, both slightly.**
  - The water cycle: it was only rebuilt in round 1, before most changes. Its old version had a better picture sort.
  - The baptism: one beat asked about the promises before they'd been taught, and the reviewer missed it.
- **The judge's remaining worries:**
  - "tell your partner" is still common, though less so in the round-5 lessons;
  - reading can be heavy in RE and history main tasks;
  - drawing and marking only turn up where the subject invites them (rivers, food chains), not everywhere.
- **Nearly every lesson now has one named character's claim to judge.** They're no longer always wrong, but the judge wondered whether one in every lesson becomes a habit. That's your call: I haven't changed it.
- **Picture-based beats** (circle the clues, finish the picture) often need a printed stick-in piece, so they add a little printing.

## Update, 29 September (after your correction)

- **The rule is now about effective, not quick.** "Make every child do the thinking, and take away only the reading and writing that is not the learning." Finishing a sentence and sorting cards children read are named as good Do beats. An A-or-B choice is only used when the choice itself is the hard part; otherwise the beat gets more decisions or the because.
- **Claims:** fully right when either would do. A named claim is used only when a voice matters; otherwise the misunderstanding goes into the task. A claim asking for a reason gets a starter on the case's own evidence, or a guiding question, plus two or three taught words. There's never a generic "I think Jack is ___ because ___", and never a starter that picks the verdict.
- **Retest:**
  - River: "A or B" became an S and an arrow on three new rivers.
  - Water cycle: two finish-the-sentence beats and an ordering task.
  - Anglo-Saxons: fix the wrong sentence, and a Roman fort as evidence.
  - Three of the four were approved. The baptism was sent back for a picture giveaway the reviewer spotted.
  - Two claim tests: Sofia is fully right, with a word bank. The cholera lesson used no claim at all and put the misunderstanding in a sort and a prediction.
