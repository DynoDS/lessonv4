Step 1 of the streamline method in `streamline-plan.md`, for one topic: how a
lesson opens and closes, and what it leaves children with. Nothing in the
plugin was changed to make it.

**Status (24 September 2026).** Every group is listed (A to N). The list has
had its independent check, and every verified finding is in it; nine of the
first twelve decisions are settled without a question and three remain.

**Snapshot.** The plugin's working tree on the night of 23 September 2026:
lesson-v4 4.2.287 (commit `79426973`) with the success-criteria change
(4.2.288) sitting uncommitted on top. Quotes are taken from that working tree,
not from a commit, and line numbers are the line in the working tree where the

and the words are what `streamline-tools/check-ledger-quotes.py` checks, and every «quoted»
passage is the file's own words.

**What counts as this topic here.** Everything the plugin says about:

- **the starter:** what it is for (recall of earlier learning, a prerequisite,
  and when a question about today may open instead), its pitch, what each
  subject retrieves, its forms and timing, how it is shown and answered on the
  board, slide 1 itself, what the design records, and what the reviewer and the
  programs check;
- **sticky knowledge:** what counts as a lasting takeaway, how many, the test
  that it is today's learning, how it is worded, where it appears (the Teach
  slide, practice slides, the sheet, the wall) and how it is referenced;
- **the Apply slide** (and the dialogic Reflect that takes its slot), and
  **practising a test question**, with every trace of the test-question starter
  the teacher removed on 20 and 21 September 2026, marked as such;
- **the lesson's end and what it hands on:** the purposeful ending, exit
  tickets, a lesson split in two, the previous lesson's folder, and what a later
  lesson may ask children to bring back.

**What belongs to a neighbouring topic.** Quick checks (`QC-`) owns quick checks
inside the lesson and the retrieval-practice rules; the Teach then Do rhythm
(`TD-`) owns the beats in between and a Teach slide's board; vocabulary
(`VOC-`) owns word introductions; assumed knowledge (`AK-`) owns what earlier
lessons may be assumed to have taught; success criteria (`SC-`) owns the
criteria panel that a sticky fact may share; worksheets (`WS-`) owns the sheet.
Their rules met here are rows marked "shared", with that ledger's row ID where
one exists. The rest of `preferences.md` is the other topic 7 list (`PF-`),

## Decisions for Daniel

Three questions, each a real pull between your own rules. Each says what the
plugin says now, what I think and what I suggest; "yes" takes the suggestion.

3. **When may the starter be a question about today instead of recall?**
   - **What it says now.** Your Starters section gives three answers. It says "A
     starter is the recap that primes the lesson", and a prediction or the
     framing of today's task belongs to its own beat. It allows an open
     explore-and-discuss opening "when the retrieval would be empty", and, when
     your brief says children already find the surface easy, opening "with the
     lesson's own big question as short exploratory discussion". But its subject
     list lets a history, geography or science starter be "a hook question the
     lesson will answer" with no condition. (Maths already keeps its recall
     starter and puts a real-world hook beside it.) (SA-B03 to B05, B19, C01, C04,
     B10)
   - **What I think.** The unconditioned line lets a Year 4 history lesson open
     on "Why did Tudor children work?" and never bring back last week's Tudor
     timeline.
   - **What I suggest.** The history, geography and science line takes the same
     two cases the section already names: a question about today opens instead
     of recall only when the recall would be empty, or when your brief says the
     surface is already easy. Either way it stays a question children think and
     talk about, not a prediction or the task explained.
   - **Question:** yes, or should that hook question stay open with no
     condition?

7. **Should the working wall keep a sticky fact's exact words?**
   - **What it says now.** The designer writes a sticky fact once and uses the
     same words on the Teach slide, a later practice slide, the worksheet and the
     Apply, because "same phrase as reference later strengthens trace"; it does
     not name the wall. The wall's own rule treats a sticky fact as prose "nobody
     is matching word for word", so it may be tightened to fit its card (about 62
     characters beside a picture). For definitions you decided the wall uses the
     lesson's words and shortens only when one genuinely cannot fit, as a whole
     sentence; for success criteria, that the wall never rewords a step. (SA-H08,
     I04 to I07, I29)
   - **What I think.** A sticky fact is the sentence children are meant to keep,
     and the wall is where they meet it again next week; a different version
     there is a second one to remember.
   - **What I suggest.** Treat sticky facts like definitions: the lesson's own
     words on the wall, shortened only when they genuinely cannot fit, and then
     still a whole sentence a teacher would say.
   - **Question:** yes, or should the wall keep its freedom to tighten them?

9. **Leaving out the Apply in a lesson that taught an idea** (the first list's 9
   and 10).
   - **What it says now.** A lesson with no Apply must say why, and the designer
     is handed a ready-made reason: "Your Turn and answers is sufficient ... no
     distinct synthesis task is needed". Your Apply rules say that reading the
     Apply only as pulling things together "is how a lesson that earned one
     loses it": when a lesson taught an idea, like significance, the ending can
     take it to a case it never showed or turn the question round ("Imagine he
     had never campaigned ..."), and it "takes two minutes". The reviewer opens
     your Apply rules only when an Apply "may be unearned or repeat Your Turn",
     never when one was left out. (SA-J03, J05, J17, J19, J48, M13)
   - **What I think.** The ready-made reason lets a designer skip the check your
     Shaftesbury lesson needed. Often the answer is already there: in a knowledge
     lesson that names an idea, the Practise is the idea on evidence children
     have not seen, so the reason can simply say so. The reviewer must not start
     demanding an Apply slide in every such lesson (one of its test cases forbids
     that).
   - **What I suggest.** The ready-made reason stays for a lesson whose learning
     is a fact or a method. A lesson that named an idea says in its reason where
     the idea met a case it was not taught on (usually the Practise), or earns an
     Apply that takes it there. The reviewer opens your Apply rules when a lesson
     named an idea, has no Apply, and its reason does not say where that
     happened.
   - **Question:** yes?
